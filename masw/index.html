Aberdeen sits on a complex geological boundary where Dalradian metamorphic rocks meet Devonian Old Red Sandstone, capped by variable glacial till deposits from the last Devensian glaciation. The city's expansion westwards beyond the A90 bypass and the ongoing energy transition developments at the Energy Transition Zone in Altens are pushing foundations onto terrain where the depth to competent rock shifts from less than 3 m near Rubislaw to over 25 m in the Dee river valley. BS EN 1998-1:2004 and the UK National Annex require a defensible VS30 value for seismic site classification of any structure exceeding 15 m height or occupancy class III and above. Our field crew runs MASW lines across Aberdeen's urban corridors using 24-channel seismographs and 4.5 Hz geophones, extracting dispersion curves that resolve shear wave velocity profiles down to 30 m without the access constraints of borehole methods. When granite head is shallow, the velocity contrast against the overlying boulder clay is sharp and the fundamental-mode dispersion curve picks up the bedrock interface within the first 15 m of the array — a signature we calibrate against CPT refusal data from adjacent investigation points to tighten the inversion model.

A VS30 value below 180 m/s in Aberdeen's harbour silts triggers a Type E classification and a 20% increase in the elastic design spectrum under Eurocode 8 — a cost driver no structural engineer wants to discover at detailed design stage.

Our approach and scope

A four-storey residential block on the former Broadford Works site required a VS30 value before Building Control would sign off the SER certification. The ground investigation logbook showed 2.5 m of made ground over 8 m of soft silty clay, sitting on a weathered granite surface at 10.5 m depth — a textbook Type D to C transition profile. We deployed two 46 m linear spreads with 2 m geophone spacing and a 10 kg sledgehammer source, stacking five blows per shot point to suppress tram and bus vibration from Mounthooly Way. The dispersion image showed a clean fundamental mode from 4 Hz to 28 Hz, and the inverted 1D VS profile gave a VS30 of 232 m/s, placing the site firmly in ground type D under BS EN 1998-1 Table 3.1. Because the client needed a foundation design check as well, we paired the seismic line with a single SPT borehole at the array midpoint — the N60 values from the clay layer correlated within 8% of the MASW-derived shear wave velocity using the Hasancebi & Ulusay (2007) regression for fine-grained soils, giving the structural engineer confidence in both the site classification and the bearing capacity assessment.

Site-specific factors

In Aberdeen, the biggest pitfall we catch during MASW campaigns is mode misidentification when a stiff glacial till layer overlies soft buried channel deposits. The first higher mode can mimic the fundamental mode between 10 Hz and 20 Hz, producing an inverted VS profile that overestimates the near-surface stiffness by 30% or more and pushes the site into a more favourable ground type than reality. We check every dispersion image against the borehole log and run forward-modelled synthetic seismograms when the stratigraphy is ambiguous — a step omitted in cheaper surveys that rely on automatic picking algorithms. Another local issue is the Union Street granite ridge: shallow bedrock east of Holburn Junction produces a dominant air-coupled wave on the geophone records that contaminates the low-frequency end of the spectrum below 6 Hz. Without a 2D FK filter applied during processing, the fundamental mode below 8 Hz becomes unreadable and the VS30 value carries a ±50 m/s uncertainty band — unacceptable for a Eurocode 8 site classification where the difference between type B and type C hangs on 20 m/s.

Common questions

How much does a MASW survey cost for a typical Aberdeen residential plot?

For a single-building plot requiring one or two MASW arrays with VS30 reporting to Eurocode 8, the fee ranges from £1,430 to £2,850 depending on array length, access constraints, and whether supplementary probing is needed for velocity calibration. Sites with hardstanding access and daytime working hours fall toward the lower end; traffic management on arterial roads or night-time noise restrictions push toward the upper end.

What depth does a MASW survey reach in Aberdeen's glacial till?

With a 46 m active-source spread and 4.5 Hz geophones, we reliably resolve shear wave velocity to 30 m depth across most of Aberdeen's till profiles. Where the till is unusually thick — for example, north of the River Don where buried channels exceed 35 m depth — we switch to a combined active-passive acquisition using 60 seconds of ambient noise recording to extend the dispersion curve below 5 Hz and reach 40 to 45 m penetration. The limiting factor is not the method but the lateral homogeneity assumption; we keep array lengths under 60 m to respect the 1D approximation.

Is MASW accepted by Aberdeen City Council Building Standards for seismic site classification?

Yes, it is the most commonly specified geophysical method for VS30 determination in Aberdeen because it is non-invasive and faster than crosshole or downhole seismic testing. Our reports reference BS EN 1998-1 §3.2.1 and include the full processing workflow — raw shot gathers, dispersion spectra, inversion parameters, and misfit curves — so the Building Standards officer can verify the ground type assignment directly. We have supplied MASW-based site classifications for over 60 projects across the city since 2018, and the reports have been accepted without objection on every submission.
